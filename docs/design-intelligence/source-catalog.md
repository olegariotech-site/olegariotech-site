# Source Catalog

This catalog records **what to learn**, not what to clone.

## Framer

Best for:

- cinematic dark marketing surfaces;
- portfolio/showcase rhythm;
- one strong visual idea per band;
- disciplined primary/secondary CTA contrast;
- atmospheric color used as an event, not wallpaper.

Borrow:

- dark-canvas confidence;
- large visual breathing room;
- strong showcase cards;
- restrained accent outside hero moments.

Avoid:

- proprietary fonts;
- copying gradient signatures;
- making every section a spectacle.

Good OT uses:

- portfolio/cases;
- premium hero;
- visual demo sections;
- creative businesses.

---

## Linear

Best for:

- quiet precision;
- dense but readable product/service information;
- surface hierarchy;
- subtle borders;
- states and navigation;
- technical credibility.

Borrow:

- low-noise dark surfaces;
- clear information hierarchy;
- careful selected/hover/focus states;
- restrained accent color.

Avoid:

- making a local business look like SaaS software;
- over-dense copy;
- copying Linear's lavender identity.

Good OT uses:

- service grids;
- process/method;
- technical proof;
- admin/utility areas;
- professional-service websites.

---

## Apple

Best for:

- product or offer as protagonist;
- whitespace as hierarchy;
- visual sequencing;
- section-by-section storytelling;
- strong mobile simplification.

Borrow:

- one subject per scene;
- spacious product presentation;
- reduce UI chrome around important media;
- let image/product carry weight instead of decoration.

Avoid:

- copying Apple typography/photography;
- empty luxury without commercial information;
- excessive scroll theatrics.

Good OT uses:

- hero product;
- premium catalog item;
- before/after;
- case-story sequence;
- product launches.

---

## Stripe

Best for:

- explaining complex offers clearly;
- benefit + proof + CTA structure;
- layered information;
- commercial trust;
- pricing/feature comparison.

Borrow:

- commercial clarity before decoration;
- progressive technical detail;
- strong CTA hierarchy;
- proof adjacent to claims.

Avoid:

- enterprise complexity where a local customer needs simplicity;
- pseudo-technical diagrams that explain nothing.

Good OT uses:

- service pages;
- proposals/offer landing pages;
- pricing explanation;
- diagnostic journeys.

---

## Webflow

Best for:

- product demonstration;
- browser/device mockups;
- explain-while-showing;
- portfolio/capability communication.

Borrow:

- show the result while explaining the service;
- use UI/site screenshots as proof;
- alternate explanation and demonstration.

Avoid:

- generic website-builder visual language;
- excessive browser frames around every image.

Good OT uses:

- site-building service pages;
- OT portfolio;
- case studies;
- before/after redesign storytelling.

---

## Shopify

Best for:

- products, collections and catalog hierarchy;
- ecommerce confidence;
- merchant/product storytelling;
- clear product actions.

Borrow:

- category architecture;
- product-card discipline;
- merchandising priority;
- price/action clarity.

Avoid:

- ecommerce UI when the client only needs WhatsApp/catalog;
- checkout complexity without a real checkout.

Good OT uses:

- floriculture/cestaria;
- mini markets;
- food catalogs;
- product-driven local commerce.

---

## Airbnb

Best for:

- photography-led discovery;
- category navigation;
- card grids;
- local/experience-oriented content;
- trust through real imagery.

Borrow:

- photo-first cards;
- simple category exploration;
- generous whitespace around imagery;
- clear metadata hierarchy.

Avoid:

- cloning marketplace mechanics for a single business;
- rounded-everything without purpose;
- using lifestyle photography as fake proof.

Good OT uses:

- restaurants;
- hospitality;
- venues;
- local experiences;
- food/product discovery.

---

## Resend

Best for:

- premium dark restraint;
- editorial typography;
- atmospheric glow;
- hairline borders;
- technical content that still feels high-end.

Borrow:

- dark editorial confidence;
- glow only as atmosphere;
- border-based depth instead of heavy shadows;
- strong typographic anchor.

Avoid:

- proprietary fonts;
- making every business look like a developer tool.

Good OT uses:

- OT itself;
- technology;
- premium professional services;
- dark brand identities.

---

## Adobe Spectrum / accessible component systems

Best for:

- component anatomy;
- interaction states;
- accessibility;
- predictable behavior;
- density/variant thinking.

Borrow:

- explicit state contracts;
- semantic controls;
- keyboard/focus requirements;
- component usage guidance.

Avoid:

- importing a full enterprise component system when native HTML/CSS is enough.

Good OT uses:

- every interactive project.
